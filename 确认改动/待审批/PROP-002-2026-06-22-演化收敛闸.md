## One sentence

Measured framework growth is structurally one-way: permanent meta-rules rose from 6 to 23 without reductions, ADRs reached 038 without retirement, and qualified P0 candidates remained pending for about a month. Three passages in the evolution philosophy call complexity a debt but provide no measurement. Add a lightweight evolution-metrics table to `04_台账`, updated with N figures per RETRO, and one mandatory question: when permanent rules grow in two successive RETRO intervals without retirement, explicitly consider rules to retire, merge, or move to a lower governance level.

## Background and motivation

The triggering moment was rank 12 of the multidimensional dogfood audit on 2026-06-22. Line 105 of `演化哲学.md` explicitly rejects indifference to framework complexity. Lines 13 and 27 likewise say complexity is debt and framework debt is harder to dismantle than code debt. All three were qualitative statements with no threshold, metric, or dashboard. RETRO-023 had already identified this lack of instrumentation.

Four measured perspectives support the finding:

- **Meta-rule pool:** the permanent count rose monotonically from 6 to 23 over about 3.5 months, March through June 14, 2026, without any reduction. Growth accelerated: v3.7→v3.8 increased 16→22, six rules in ten days; June added seven net rules. The maintenance process in `元规则池.md`, lines 46–50, defined addition, promotion, rejection, and deferral, but no retirement, demotion, or repeal. None of the 23 rules had been removed or demoted since v1.0. Merging CU+DD and DH+DK+DF compressed rows without reducing underlying issues: 23 rows represented 26 letter identifiers.
- **Candidate pool:** 17 candidates remained: P0=3, P1=8, P2=3, P3=3. CD, CN, and CP were already marked P0, with implemented proposal evidence and sufficient cross-sprint accumulation, but still awaited promotion at the RETRO/decision stage. They had been qualified but unpromoted since the v3.1 pool was established on 2026-05-22, roughly one month; their earliest issue history reached about three months. The count was not monotonic: it once fell from a peak of 19 to 10 in v3.7, but after v3.8 cleanup it rebounded to 17 in v3.9. Reductions were occasional actions rather than a standing mechanism.
- **ADRs:** 001→038 comprised 38 consecutive, unreused numbers over roughly 37 days, about one per day. The observed state machine had only two states: 35 current and three superseded. There was no separate superseded, retired, or repealed archive. Superseded ADRs 003, 007, and 011 retained their numbers and rows in the main index. Replacement did not mean retirement; file and index counts only grew.
- **RETROs and complexity instrumentation:** 001→023 added 23 retrospectives in about six weeks, without retirement. Six types of framework trigger events could accelerate the cycle as the framework grew. `check-retro-cadence.ps1` issued only nonblocking exit-5 reminders and counted day batches rather than individual changes. Six framework changes occurred on 2026-06-22, but recording RETRO-023 that day made sinceCount=0 and displayed green. Same-day accumulation was invisible. Its default Threshold=2 also differed from README's N=3. Commits, CHANGELOG entries, and individual changes were not one-to-one measures, and no authoritative complexity instrument existed.

The five aggregate views in `04_台账`—version timeline, sprint cadence, issue overview, project lessons, and audit coverage—measured output rather than framework size. A directory-wide search for complexity, metrics, instrumentation, convergence, retirement, or simplification found one unrelated AppData-retirement reference. The three expansion questions in the evolution philosophy, lines 49–59, governed only admission: none asked about complexity budgets or retirement conditions. Gates prevented additions without triggering simplification. Convergence remained aspirational language such as "continue converging" or "v4.0 final state." The closest retirement-like mechanism, issue BK's reduced monitoring in `Sprint节奏.md:96`, applied to one issue rather than the whole framework.

## Proposed approach

Keep this lightweight: one metrics view, one mandatory rule, and an optional later retirement state.

1. **Add an evolution-metrics table to `04_台账`.** This is an aggregate view, not a schema change. Update N figures when each RETRO closes, arranging existing size indicators into a timeline. Start with five columns: permanent meta-rule count; candidate count and age in days of the oldest candidate; current ADR count and cumulative superseded-but-not-retired count; framework-change batches in the RETRO interval; and the backlog count of qualified-but-unpromoted candidates. Make trends visible during a RETRO.
2. **Add one convergence rule to the RETRO template/evolution philosophy's required questions.** When the net permanent-rule increase is positive for two consecutive RETRO intervals and no rule was retired during them, the current RETRO must explicitly answer: "Should any rule be retired, merged, or moved to a lower governance level?" "No, because X" is acceptable; omission is not. This remains a reminder, not a blocking gate.
3. **Optional Phase 2: retirement/superseded states for ADRs and candidates.** Provide a mechanism that supports reduction, such as moving superseded ADRs into a retirement section or marking a candidate repealed. This has a larger impact and is excluded from this proposal's initial scope.

At the data/documentation level, add one pointer-and-timeline aggregate view without duplicating content, consistent with `INDEX.md` line 11, and one required RETRO question. This complements `check-retro-cadence.ps1`: that script asks whether a retrospective is due; the metrics view determines what to inspect during one. It may encourage consistent units for individual changes, batches, and commits, but that alignment is not mandatory in this proposal.

## Estimated impact and cost

- Effort: **S–M**, for one table and one required question. The optional retirement states would raise this to M–L.
- Affected files: approximately 3–5; a new `04_台账` view, its INDEX link, the RETRO/README template question, and optionally ADR README state documentation and candidate-pool maintenance guidance.
- Risk: low to medium. The metrics view could itself become another maintenance burden and ironically increase complexity.
- Schema change: **No.** This is a documentation/process change; the ledger is an aggregate view rather than a data model.

## Alternatives

- **A, recommended:** a five-column metrics table, one required convergence question, and retirement states reserved for optional Phase 2. Lightweight, triggerable, and inexpensive to maintain.
- **B, minimal:** only the required question, without a table. Cheapest, but without visible trends, two successive growth intervals require manual counting and may be missed, as happened with CD/CN/CP promotion.
- **C, comprehensive:** A plus immediate ADR/candidate retirement states, unified change/batch/commit units, and complexity ceilings. Stronger evidence, but a much larger scope that contradicts this proposal's exploratory stage; split it into separate work.

## Likely reasons for rejection

The strongest objection is that maintaining a table at every RETRO adds the very complexity it is intended to reduce. Built-in mitigations are a hard limit of five columns; copying figures directly from existing authoritative sources such as `元规则池.md` frontmatter, the ADR README, and the RETRO index instead of calculating new measures; and triggering no extra convergence question unless growth spans two RETROs. The table should be a readout, not a new ledger-maintenance project. If the benefit still does not justify the cost, fall back to option B.

Another objection is that individual-change, batch, and commit units should be aligned first. That would require a prerequisite proposal; this table provisionally uses batches.

## Out of scope

- Deleting or retiring existing meta-rules, ADRs, or RETROs. Actual retirement decisions follow individually from later triggered retrospectives.
- Blocking gates; reminders remain nonblocking, consistent with check-retro-cadence exit 5.
- Implementing ADR/candidate retirement states in this round; they remain optional Phase 2.
- Mandatory alignment of individual-change, batch, and commit counts; track that separately.
- Changing the three expansion questions or the six framework trigger-event types. This adds a trigger for reduction to an otherwise one-way growth structure.
